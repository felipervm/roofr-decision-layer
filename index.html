A pipeline can look busy.
Is it ready for action?

Roofr already offers performance reporting, record drill-downs, exports, and CRM automations. This case starts with the records that inform those tools.

My question is more specific: could a focused review of stale and incomplete CRM records help managers decide what to do next?

What this does not prove: These flags are generated from fictional data under an intentionally simple 10-day rule. They do not establish a Roofr product gap, customer behavior, recoverable revenue, or the right threshold for a real roofing team.

How the numbers are defined

Source: 1,200 fictional jobs generated in Python with seed 418. Every metric, preview and filter uses the same CSV. Snapshot: October 19, 2026.

Open: Lead received, Qualified, Inspection booked, or Proposal sent. Won and Lost are excluded.

Activity review: Open jobs with at least 10 days since last activity. This illustrative threshold is not a Roofr default. The simulation intentionally creates a substantial share of old records so the checks can be demonstrated; it is not an estimate of real-world Roofr exception rates.

Missing next step: Qualified, Inspection booked, or Proposal sent records with an empty next-step field. These flags can overlap with inactivity.

Lost without reason: Lost jobs with no recorded loss reason ÷ all lost jobs in the selected source. Counts describe the simulated snapshot, not conversion trends or causal effects.

Simulated records, not Roofr results. No internal customer data, verified product gap, or revenue uplift is claimed.

03 / HOW I APPROACH THE WORK

Show the reasoning.
Make it reproducible.

A useful analysis should survive a second look. The source code, data-generation rules, and checks are open for review.

Explore the repository ↗
01

Start with the product and the user.

Read Roofr's public product materials. Frame a narrow hypothesis that complements its existing dashboards and CRM automations.

02

Build a model we can inspect.

Generate 1,200 synthetic records in Python with seed 418. Define open stages, a 10-day inactivity rule, and explicit denominators.

03

Check the data before the story.

Validate unique IDs, stage totals, positive values, and loss-reason completeness. Keep assumptions visible.

04

Translate findings into a test.

Propose a pilot. Measure whether exceptions get resolved faster and whether the review creates unnecessary work.

04 / WHAT I'D TEST WITH THE TEAM

Four weeks. One focused hypothesis.

Can an exception-first review improve follow-through without adding admin?

WEEK 01

Define the signal.

Agree on eligible stages and inactivity thresholds with product and support. Manually inspect false positives.

WEEK 02

Establish the baseline.

Measure unresolved exceptions and time to next action. Segment by stage and source.

WEEKS 03–04

Test the review.

Compare with baseline and, where feasible, a similar untreated group. Track resolution time, loss-reason completeness, false positives, and review burden. Stop or simplify if the flags are mostly noise.

A proposed experiment, not a measured result. A before/after comparison alone would not establish causality.
